The tax engine lives in `@fiscode/tax` as **pure** functions: no React, no DOM, no DB. It takes a
plain bundle and returns plain estimates.

## Strategy seam

```ts
interface TaxStrategy {
  compute(input: TaxInput, year: YearConfig): TaxEstimate;
  quarterly(input: TaxInput, year: YearConfig, method: "even" | "annualized"): QuarterlyPlan;
}
```

`engine.computeForYear(year)` picks a strategy based on the **active entity record for that year**,
not a global. Today there are two strategies:

- `SoleProprietorStrategy` — full implementation. SE tax (with wage-base + additional Medicare),
  QBI, federal brackets per filing status, state, quarterly + annualized + safe-harbor.
- `SCorpStrategy` — `NotImplemented` stub. The interface exists so adding it later is data + an
  implementation, not a refactor. Excluded from the UI by the `isSCorp` flag.

## Year config

`packages/tax/src/config/<year>.ts` holds the constants that change annually (brackets, SS wage
base, mileage rates, standard deduction, QBI thresholds, state rates). Each sourced constant has
its primary source in a comment beside it; anything not yet checked is marked
`// todo: verify against IRS/SSA for <year>`.

### QBI deduction

20% of qualified business income, at most 20% of taxable income before the deduction. Above the
threshold, with no W-2 wages or property, it shrinks in proportion to how far taxable income is
into the phase-in band, and is $0 at the band's end. From 2026, anyone with $1,000 or more of QBI
gets at least $400 ([26 U.S.C. 199A(i)](https://uscode.house.gov), added by Pub. L. 119-21 §70105).

### Self-employed health insurance

Premiums entered under the "Self-employed health insurance" expense category are not a Schedule C
expense. They are an adjustment on Schedule 1 line 17, figured on
[Form 7206](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f7206.pdf):

- **The limit:** line 14 is "the smaller of line 3 [the premiums] or line 13". Line 13 is the
  business's net profit, less the deductible half of SE tax, less SEP/SIMPLE contributions.
  fiscode doesn't model retirement contributions, so it deducts at most net profit minus half the
  SE tax.
- **SE tax:** "You can't subtract the self-employed health insurance deduction when figuring net
  earnings for your self-employment tax"
  ([instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7206)). SE tax is figured on the full net
  profit.
- **What it lowers:** AGI, so federal and Utah income tax.
- **QBI:** it lowers QBI too. Reg. 1.199A-3(b)(1)(vi) counts "the self-employed health insurance
  deduction under section 162(l)" as attributable to the business.

Not modelled:

- "You can't take the deduction for any month you were eligible to participate in any employer
  (including your spouse's) subsidized health plan". fiscode has no input for that eligibility, so
  leave out premiums for those months.
- Marketplace plans with the premium tax credit. The
  [Form 7206 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i7206.pdf) say: "See Pub. 974 if the
  insurance plan was considered to be established under your business and was obtained through the
  Marketplace, and advance payments of the premium tax credit were made or you are claiming the
  premium tax credit." Under [Pub 974](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p974.pdf) the deduction and
  the credit are figured together; fiscode does neither. Enter only what you paid yourself, and
  expect your preparer to adjust.

### Startup costs (Section 195)

An expense ticked "Startup expense" and paid before the self-employment start date is a startup
cost. These are deducted together in the year the business begins, not the year they were paid.
[IRC §195(b)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/26/195): "the taxpayer shall be allowed a
deduction for the taxable year in which the active trade or business begins in an amount equal to
the lesser of— (i) the amount of start-up expenditures with respect to the active trade or
business, or (ii) $5,000, reduced (but not below zero) by the amount by which such start-up
expenditures exceed $50,000, and (B) the remainder of such start-up expenditures shall be allowed
as a deduction ratably over the 180-month period beginning with the month in which the active trade
or business begins."

- The start-year amount counts on the SE start date. The rest counts 1/180 a month, from the start
  month on. Each year gets its months, and each quarterly period gets the months it covers.
- Example, $12,000 paid before a March 2, 2026 start: $5,000 + 10 months × $38.89 = $5,388.89 in
  2026, then $466.67 a year, ending in February 2041.
- The year-end packet shows the year's deduction and the total paid on its own line.
- The yearly CSV carries the startup rows, at their full amount, in the year the business begins.
  Above the expenses, a `#` note line gives the full amount and the amount deductible that year.
  Each later year's file has a note with that year's amortization. So the amount column reconciles
  with the app.
- Each year's amount is rounded on the running total, so the years add up to exactly what was paid.
- A startup-ticked expense paid on or after the SE start is an ordinary expense of its own year.
- Equipment and self-employed health insurance are never startup costs, even if ticked. Equipment
  is placed in service (see Equipment), and health insurance is a Schedule 1 adjustment.

Not modelled: organizational costs (§248), which have their own $5,000 deduction.

### Equipment

fiscode deducts equipment in full in the year it counts. Two rules decide which year, and when a
note appears:

- **Placed in service.** Equipment counts in the year it is placed in service, never before the
  self-employment start. [Pub 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946): "You begin to
  depreciate your property when you place it in service for use in your trade or business"; and
  property converted to business use is placed in service "on the date of the change".
  - Equipment bought before the business began is placed in service on the SE start date.
  - The expense form shows a "Placed in service" date for equipment, prefilled with the SE start
    when the purchase date is before it.
  - With no date stored, equipment counts on its purchase date, or on the SE start if bought before
    it, the same date the form shows. So an imported row counts the same before and after it's
    opened and saved.
  - Equipment is never a Section 195 startup cost, even ticked "Startup expense". IRC
    §195(c)(1)(B) limits start-up expenditures to amounts which "if paid or incurred in connection
    with the operation of an existing active trade or business ... would be allowable as a deduction
    for the taxable year in which paid or incurred". Equipment is capital property, so it's placed
    in service instead.
    The forms clear the tick for equipment. A row from a CSV or an older save can still carry it;
    the estimate counts those rows for the year (`startupIgnoredForEquipment`) so the app can say
    the tick is ignored. No figure changes.
  - A placed-in-service date only applies to equipment; on any other row (possible only from a
    CSV) it's ignored.
- **Section 179.** Deducting equipment in full is fine up to the de minimis safe harbor: "up to
  $2,500 per item or invoice" ([Pub 334](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p334)). Above that it
  assumes a Section 179 election. The dashboard notes each equipment expense over $2,500 without the
  "§179 candidate" tick. No number changes.

**Equipment you owned for personal use before the business:** enter its basis, not what you paid.
[Pub 946](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p946.pdf), Property changed from personal use: "If you held
property for personal use and later use it in your business or income-producing activity, your
depreciable basis is the lesser of the following. 1. The FMV of the property on the date of the
change in use. 2. Your original cost or other basis adjusted as follows". fiscode counts the amount
entered, so enter the lower of the fair market value when you started using it for the business and
what you paid. Equipment bought for the business just before it began is worth about what you paid.

Not modelled: depreciation (MACRS) for equipment you don't expense, and the Section 179 limits.

### Utah income tax

Utah taxes income starting from federal AGI, not federal taxable income (TC-40 line 7). The tax is
4.45% of that, minus the nonrefundable taxpayer tax credit
([Utah Code 59-10-1018](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title59/Chapter10/C59-10-S1018_2026050620260506.pdf)):
6% of (the federal standard deduction + the personal exemption per dependent), reduced by 1.3% of
income above the filing-status base, and never below zero. The 2026 exemption and base amounts
come from Utah's draft 2026 TC-40 and may change when the final form is posted.

### Home office

Two methods, each counted only for the days the office is in use within the year, and never
before the self-employment start date:

- **Simplified:** $5 per square foot, at most 300 sq ft ($1,500 a year). Part of a year, or a
  change of area, uses the average monthly allowable square footage
  ([Pub 587](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p587.pdf), "Part-year use or area changes"): "adding
  the amount of allowable square feet you used in each month and dividing the sum by 12", with at
  most 300 sq ft in any month, and "if your qualified business use was less than 15 days in a
  month, you must use -0- for that month". Pub 587's Example 1, 420 sq ft from July 20, gives 125
  sq ft, so $625.

  Two simplified configs in one year are the same home with a change of area: the instructions
  allow the simplified method for one home only ("If you had more than one home during the year
  that you used for business, you can use the simplified method for only one home"). The 15-day
  test is on the month's business use across both: a month counts when the days add up to 15 or
  more, at the area in use for most of those days (on a tie, the later one), at most 300 sq ft.

- **Actual** ([Form 8829](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/f8829--dft.pdf), 2026 draft; lines
  match the [2025 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8829)): the business percentage
  is office area ÷ home area (line 3, "Divide line 1 by line 2"). The monthly rent or mortgage
  interest, utilities and insurance (column (b)) are multiplied by it (line 24, "Multiply line 23,
  column (b), by line 7"). Part-year use: "you can deduct only the expenses paid or incurred for
  the portion of the year you used your home for business" (instructions, Columns (a) and (b)). An
  office larger than the home is rejected and deducts nothing.

Both are limited by the business's gross income: the deduction is at most the tentative profit
before it (Form 8829 line 8, Schedule C line 29; line 27 "Enter the smaller of line 15 or line
26"; [Pub 587](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p587) "Gross income limitation", which applies to
the simplified method too). What the limit cuts depends on the method:

- **Actual:** carried to next year (Form 8829 line 43). fiscode reports it but does not carry it
  across years.
- **Simplified:** lost. "Amount in excess of gross income limitation may not be carried over"
  ([IRS, simplified option](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/simplified-option-for-home-office-deduction)).

**Whose income counts:** every home office, one or several, is limited by the profit from the
income, expenses and mileage dated within its own days in use. Pub 587: "If some of the income is
from a place of business other than your home, you must first determine the part of your gross
income from the business use of your home". For more than one home, the Line 8 instructions say:
"include only the income earned and the deductions attributable to that income during the period
you owned the home". So:

- A home used from July 1 is limited by the profit dated from July 1. Income earned before it
  counts toward no home office.
- Each date belongs to one home, for both its deduction and its profit: when a move is entered as
  "ends Jul 1" and "starts Jul 1", the new home has Jul 1. Any overlap is treated as a move to the
  later home, which owns the shared days.
- A config that deducts nothing (no costs, or an office larger than the home) has no days.
- The total never exceeds the business's whole tentative profit. Any excess is cut from the latest
  home back, and is lost or carried over by that home's method.

**Mortgage interest and real estate taxes:** fiscode assumes the standard deduction, so their
business share goes on lines 16 and 17 and is limited with the other operating expenses ("If you
claim the standard deduction, you will not include any mortgage interest or real estate taxes on
lines 10 and 11; instead, you will claim the entire business use of the home portion of those
expenses using lines 16 and 17", instructions, Lines 9, 10 and 11). An itemizer would put them on
lines 10 and 11, outside the limit; itemizing is not modelled. For an owner, enter the mortgage
interest only, not the principal.

Not modelled yet:

- **An area change in the same home** is entered as two configs, so the income limit treats it
  like a move: each config is limited by the profit dated within its own days. Pub 587 treats it
  as one home with one limit.
- **One simplified home a year** is not enforced. fiscode reads two simplified configs in one year
  as an area change in the same home; it does not stop someone entering two homes that way.
- **Real estate taxes:** no field.
- **Depreciation** for owners (lines 36–42): there is no purchase price or basis to compute it from.
- **Direct expenses** (column (a), such as painting the office): no field yet.
- **The actual-method carryover** across years (line 43 → next year's line 25).

### Quarterly estimates

The plan follows Form 2210. The lines used read the same in the
[2025 form](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f2210.pdf) and the
[2026 draft](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/f2210--dft.pdf) (created April 16, 2026):

- If the federal tax left after federal withholding is under $1,000, no installments are required
  and every card is $0 (lines 4 and 7: "If less than $1,000, stop; you don't owe a penalty"). The dashboard says
  so in one sentence.
- The year target is the required annual payment (line 9): the smaller of 90% of this year's
  federal tax and the prior-year amount, which is the safe-harbor floor. The prior-year amount
  comes from the Profile: the previous year's total tax and AGI, saved with the year they are
  from. They count only for the year after it, so a 2025 return sets the 2026 floor and is
  ignored in 2027; the dashboard then asks for the 2026 return.
  [Pub 505](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505): "100% of the total tax shown on your 2025
  return. Your 2025 tax return must cover all 12 months." "If your AGI for 2025 was more than
  $150,000 ($75,000 if your filing status for 2026 is Married filing a separate return)",
  "substitute 110% for 100%". The total tax "is the amount on Form 1040 or 1040–SR, line 24
  reduced by" refundable credits (lines 27a–32), and AGI "is the amount shown on Form 1040 or
  1040-SR, line 11b". With the tax entered and the AGI blank, fiscode uses 110%, which is safe
  either way, and says why. Left blank (a first year, no return filed, or a short prior year:
  "If you didn't file a return for 2024 or your 2024 tax year was less than 12 months, don't
  complete line 8", [Form 2210 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i2210)), the target
  is 90% of this year's. $0 is a value: a year with no tax gives a $0 floor.
- Not modelled: a joint return last year and separate returns this year. The instructions say
  "your 2024 tax is your share of the tax on the joint return"; enter your share yourself.
- Regular method: each required installment is 25% of the target (line 10).
- Annualized method (Schedule AI): each period's income, mileage, home office and other
  expenses through that period are annualized and the year's tax figured on them. Each
  installment is the smaller of the annualized amount, with any earlier reduction recaptured, and
  the regular 25% plus whatever earlier regular installments left unused (lines 21-27). The four
  always add up to the target.
- Federal withholding counts as paid a quarter at a time
  ([instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i2210.pdf)), so each card shows what is left to
  pay after it.

Notes on the quarterly plan:

- Schedule AI Part II figures self-employment tax per period with a prorated Social Security
  limit. Without W-2 wages of your own, that gives the same result as the annualized calculation
  fiscode uses. With your own W-2 wages it would differ, and fiscode does not model them yet.
- The plan is federal only, as Form 2210 is: federal income tax plus self-employment tax, minus
  federal withholding. State tax and state withholding never enter it.

### Utah prepayment

Utah has no quarterly schedule and no $1,000 test. Its
[TC-546](https://files.tax.utah.gov/tax/forms/current/tc-546.pdf) asks for a minimum by the
return's due date: the lesser of 90% of this year's Utah tax and last year's Utah tax, minus Utah
withholding ("If less than zero, a payment is not due at this time"). Unpaid, it costs "2 percent
of the unpaid tax for each month of the extension period". The return is due the same day as the
federal one ([Utah Code 59-10-514](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title59/Chapter10/59-10-S514.html)),
April 15. Last year's Utah tax comes from the Profile, under the same year rule; left blank or
from another year, fiscode uses 90% of this year's.

Not modelled yet:

- The QBI minimum assumes the owner materially participates: §199A(i) counts only QBI from an
  active business (§469(h)). A specified service business above the band would get no minimum.
- QBI for a specified service business (§199A(d)), W-2 wages and qualified property. The
  estimate treats the business as non-SSTB with no employees and no property, so above the
  threshold the deduction phases down to $0 across the band (then the $400 minimum applies).
- Utah additions to and subtractions from income, itemized deductions, and the extra exemption
  for a dependent born in the year.
- A spouse's W-2 wages and withholding count only when filing jointly; under any other status
  fiscode counts none of them. Married filing separately in a community property state (AZ, CA,
  ID, LA, NV, NM, TX, WA, WI) differs: each spouse reports half of the community income and
  claims half of the withholding on it
  ([Pub 555](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p555.pdf)). Not modelled.

`getYearConfig(year)` falls back to the nearest defined year and surfaces a console warning + UI
banner — stale data is loud, not silent.

## What fiscode does not do

- No IRS form generation. No filing.
- No actual-expense vehicle method (standard mileage only).
- No retirement / Roth UI in v1 (schema + CSV + flags only).
