The tax engine lives in `@fiscode/tax` as **pure** functions: no React, no DOM, no DB. It takes a
plain bundle and returns plain estimates.

## Strategy seam

```ts
interface TaxStrategy {
  compute(input: TaxInput, year: YearConfig): TaxEstimate;
  quarterly(input: TaxInput, year: YearConfig, method: "even" | "annualized"): QuarterlyPlan;
}
```

`engine.computeForYear(year)` picks a strategy based on the **active entity record for that year**,
not a global. Today there are two strategies:

- `SoleProprietorStrategy` — full implementation. SE tax (with wage-base + additional Medicare),
  QBI, federal brackets per filing status, state, quarterly + annualized + safe-harbor.
- `SCorpStrategy` — `NotImplemented` stub. The interface exists so adding it later is data + an
  implementation, not a refactor. Excluded from the UI by the `isSCorp` flag.

## Year config

`packages/tax/src/config/<year>.ts` holds the constants that change annually (brackets, SS wage
base, mileage rates, standard deduction, QBI thresholds, state rates). Each sourced constant has
its primary source in a comment beside it; anything not yet checked is marked
`// todo: verify against IRS/SSA for <year>`.

### QBI deduction

20% of qualified business income, at most 20% of taxable income before the deduction. Above the
threshold, with no W-2 wages or property, it shrinks in proportion to how far taxable income is
into the phase-in band, and is $0 at the band's end. From 2026, anyone with $1,000 or more of QBI
gets at least $400 ([26 U.S.C. 199A(i)](https://uscode.house.gov), added by Pub. L. 119-21 §70105).

### Utah income tax

Utah taxes income starting from federal AGI, not federal taxable income (TC-40 line 7). The tax is
4.45% of that, minus the nonrefundable taxpayer tax credit
([Utah Code 59-10-1018](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title59/Chapter10/C59-10-S1018_2026050620260506.pdf)):
6% of (the federal standard deduction + the personal exemption per dependent), reduced by 1.3% of
income above the filing-status base, and never below zero. The 2026 exemption and base amounts
come from Utah's draft 2026 TC-40 and may change when the final form is posted.

### Home office

Two methods, each prorated to the days the office is in use within the year, and never before the
self-employment start date:

- **Simplified:** $5 per square foot, at most 300 sq ft ($1,500 a year).
- **Actual** ([Form 8829](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/f8829--dft.pdf), 2026 draft; lines
  match the [2025 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8829)): the business percentage
  is office area ÷ home area (line 3, "Divide line 1 by line 2"). The monthly rent or mortgage
  interest, utilities and insurance (lines 19, 21 and 18, column (b)) are multiplied by it (line 24,
  "Multiply line 23, column (b), by line 7"). Part-year use: "you can deduct only the expenses
  paid or incurred for the portion of the year you used your home for business" (instructions,
  Columns (a) and (b)). An office larger than the home is rejected and deducts nothing.

Both are limited by the business's gross income: the deduction is at most the tentative profit
before it (Form 8829 line 8, Schedule C line 29; line 27 "Enter the smaller of line 15 or line
26"; [Pub 587](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p587) "Gross income limitation", which applies to
the simplified method too). The excess is Form 8829 line 43's carryover to next year; fiscode
reports it but does not carry it.

Not modelled yet:

- **Mortgage interest outside the limit.** Deductible mortgage interest (line 10) is subtracted
  before the limit (line 15 = line 8 − line 14), so it is never limited. fiscode stores rent and
  mortgage as one monthly figure and limits all of it. For an owner, enter the interest only, not
  the principal.
- **Depreciation** for owners (lines 36–42): there is no purchase price or basis to compute it from.
- **Direct expenses** (column (a), such as painting the office): no field yet.
- **The carryover** of disallowed expenses to the next year (line 43 → line 25).

### Quarterly estimates

The plan follows Form 2210. The lines used read the same in the
[2025 form](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f2210.pdf) and the
[2026 draft](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/f2210--dft.pdf) (created April 16, 2026):

- If the federal tax left after federal withholding is under $1,000, no installments are required
  and every card is $0 (lines 4 and 7: "If less than $1,000, stop; you don't owe a penalty"). The dashboard says
  so in one sentence.
- The year target is the required annual payment (line 9): the smaller of 90% of this year's
  federal tax and the prior-year amount, which is the safe-harbor floor.
- Regular method: each required installment is 25% of the target (line 10).
- Annualized method (Schedule AI): each period's income, mileage, home office and other
  expenses through that period are annualized and the year's tax figured on them. Each
  installment is the smaller of the annualized amount, with any earlier reduction recaptured, and
  the regular 25% plus whatever earlier regular installments left unused (lines 21-27). The four
  always add up to the target.
- Federal withholding counts as paid a quarter at a time
  ([instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i2210.pdf)), so each card shows what is left to
  pay after it.

Notes on the quarterly plan:

- Schedule AI Part II figures self-employment tax per period with a prorated Social Security
  limit. Without W-2 wages of your own, that gives the same result as the annualized calculation
  fiscode uses. With your own W-2 wages it would differ, and fiscode does not model them yet.
- The plan is federal only, as Form 2210 is: federal income tax plus self-employment tax, minus
  federal withholding. State tax and state withholding never enter it.

### Utah prepayment

Utah has no quarterly schedule and no $1,000 test. Its
[TC-546](https://files.tax.utah.gov/tax/forms/current/tc-546.pdf) asks for a minimum by the
return's due date: the lesser of 90% of this year's Utah tax and last year's Utah tax, minus Utah
withholding ("If less than zero, a payment is not due at this time"). Unpaid, it costs "2 percent
of the unpaid tax for each month of the extension period". The return is due the same day as the
federal one ([Utah Code 59-10-514](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title59/Chapter10/59-10-S514.html)),
April 15. fiscode has no input for last year's Utah tax, so it shows 90% of this year's.

Not modelled yet:

- The QBI minimum assumes the owner materially participates: §199A(i) counts only QBI from an
  active business (§469(h)). A specified service business above the band would get no minimum.
- QBI for a specified service business (§199A(d)), W-2 wages and qualified property. The
  estimate treats the business as non-SSTB with no employees and no property, so above the
  threshold the deduction phases down to $0 across the band (then the $400 minimum applies).
- Utah additions to and subtractions from income, itemized deductions, and the extra exemption
  for a dependent born in the year.

`getYearConfig(year)` falls back to the nearest defined year and surfaces a console warning + UI
banner — stale data is loud, not silent.

## What fiscode does not do

- No IRS form generation. No filing.
- No actual-expense vehicle method (standard mileage only).
- No retirement / Roth UI in v1 (schema + CSV + flags only).
